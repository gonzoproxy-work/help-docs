# Why Proxy Speed Varies

A proxy's speed depends on what it runs on. Residential, mobile, and datacenter proxies have different connection sources, so the causes of speed differences are different too. The general rule is the same for all: a proxy can't be faster than the channel your traffic goes through.

### Residential Proxies

They run on real home devices that rent out their IP. So the speed depends on:

* The internet provider's plan. Each IP owner has their own terms: gigabit or a basic 50 Mbps plan;
* Upload speed. On many home plans it is noticeably lower than download speed;
* Home network load. If people in the home that provides the IP are watching videos or downloading files, the channel is shared and you can't use it at full capacity;
* Connection type. A wired connection is usually more stable than Wi-Fi;
* Time of day. In the evening, the load on providers' networks usually grows.

### Mobile Proxies

They run through mobile internet, and the speed is determined by the operator and by network conditions at a specific location. What affects it here:

* Operator plan and limits. Some plans cut the speed after a certain amount of traffic is used;
* Network type. 5G and 4G give different speeds, and with weak coverage the connection may switch between standards;
* Cell tower load. In crowded places and at peak hours, the speed drops;
* Signal strength. It depends on the place where the equipment is located.

Mobile proxy speed is usually less predictable than that of home proxies. It changes throughout the day.

### Datacenter Proxies

They run on servers, and the factors that affect speed are different here:

* Server channel. It is usually higher and more stable than a home one;
* Server load. If many clients use it at the same time, the channel is shared between them;
* Distance to the target service. The farther the server is from the site you need, the higher the latency.

Their speed is generally steadier and higher, but services more often recognize such addresses as server ones, so they are not suitable for some tasks.

### What to Do If Your Proxy Is Slow

1. Create a new proxy. When you recreate it, the IP changes, and with it the device or server. The new connection may turn out to be noticeably faster.
2. If you selected a provider, try removing it. Then more devices match, and you are more likely to get a fast one among them.
3. If you need a specific city, widen the filter to the region, and add the city back later if you really need it.

### What to Keep in Mind

* Speed variation between proxies doesn't mean something is broken. It is a feature of networks where connections belong to different owners.
